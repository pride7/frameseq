AI assistant

Ask for a change. Accept it like a pull request.

The Assistant panel runs Claude Code or Codex, whichever your computer has, on your own sign-in. It edits the slides, checks the layout, looks at what it drew, and lays out every edit for you to accept or reject.

  1. 1

    Ask in your own words

    The request carries the slide in view, the lines you selected, and the problems in the list, so “this slide” means something.

  2. 2

    It checks its own work

    Through frameseq mcp the agent runs the layout check and looks at pictures of the slides before it says it is done.

  3. 3

    You decide what stays

    The editor shows each change against what was there before, to accept or reject. Ctrl+Z undoes the lot.

Presenting

Two screens, your notes, and your phone as the clicker.

Press F5 and the slides fill the screen edge to edge. With a projector attached, they go to the projector, and presenter view stays on your laptop with the notes, the next slide, and a timer.

Full-screen show

F5 from the start, Shift+F5 from here. Clicks and arrow keys step through reveals, and the show ends on black.

Presenter view

Notes, the next slide, and a timer on your screen, with the slides sent to the screen you choose.

Phone remote

Scan a QR code to change slides from your phone, read the notes on it, and point a laser at the screen.

A PDF for the backup laptop

Export the whole talk or just some slides, numbered as they were, for the projector you did not plan for.

Underneath

One file that reads from top to bottom.

Every slide is a few lines of TypeScript, with no nesting and no markup. Source order is reading order, so a diff shows exactly what changed, and an AI writes it without getting lost.

talk.slides.ts 
slide("Faster every release").split("38:62")
  .notes("Median load time, measured on a mid-range phone over 4G.");
text("Median time to first tile fell by two thirds in a year.").lead();
bullets(
  "Vector tiles replaced images",
  "A cache at every edge",
  "Fonts shipped with the app",
);

right();
chart("bar", { "1.0": 840, "1.4": 610, "1.8": 430, "2.0": 280 })
  .unit(" ms")
  .colors("#0d9488");

Layout without CSS

Built for real talks

Diagrams, equations, charts, and posters.

Diagrams

Shapes and connectors that stay editable

Typesetting

And the details

Citations, icons, and QR codes

Import a .bib file and cite() as you write; each slide lists its sources at the foot. Lucide's icons come by name, and qr() puts a link your audience can scan on the last slide.
